## Fundamentals — Definition

- **Design Thinking is a method for developing creative ideas and for problem solving.**
- Design thinking puts the following things into focus:
  - **Customers** (or the **benefit**)
  - **Possibility to realize** (feasibility)
  - **Commercial viability**
- **Team selection** should be **as heterogeneous as possible** (diverse backgrounds give better ideas).
- Design thinking is an **agile method** that needs **several iterations** to reach the goal.
- Core motto: **➜ Fail fast and cheap!**

> The three focus points (customer benefit · feasibility · commercial viability) are the classic "desirability – feasibility – viability" lens of design thinking.

## The Design Thinking Process

The process moves between two "spaces":

- **Problem Space** — where you *understand the problem and the customer*.
- **Solution Space** — where you *create and test solutions*.

The six iterative steps (translated Design Thinking process [ON_DesignThinking_Ablauf]):

| # | Step | Space |
|---|------|-------|
| 1 | **Understand** | Problem space |
| 2 | **Observe** | Problem space |
| 3 | **Design Principles** (synthesis / Point-of-View) | bridge between spaces |
| 4 | **Ideation** | Solution space |
| 5 | **Prototyping** | Solution space |
| 6 | **Testing** | Solution space |

The process is **iterative** — you loop back through prototyping and testing several times until the customer is satisfied. A video example is referenced as [ON_DesignThinking_ExampleVideo].

## (1) Understand

**Content: Understand the problem and the target.**
- **Build the team.**
- What should be **newly developed**?
- What's the **problem**?
- What's the **target condition** after using the technology? — **NOT** the technical solution!
- **Who is the customer?**
- Which **environment conditions** must be taken into account?

**How to:**
- Run an **initial expert circle / brainstorming** — and **update it with each new piece of information** gathered during the design thinking process.

> Key exam point: in "Understand" you describe the **target condition / desired outcome**, *not* a technical solution.

---

## (2) Observe

**Content: Understand the customer.**
- Analysis of the **customer's will**.
- Understand the **wishes and inner needs** of the customer.
- **Collect as much information as possible.**
- What is the **actual problem** that he/she wants solved?
- What should it be able to **do**?
- But also their **personal background** (family, hobbies, …)?

**How to: Learn from people / the customer.**
- **Talk and interview** the customer.
- **Spend time** with the customer / **observe** him/her.
- Find out the relevant **peer groups**.
- **Extreme voices** raise important information compared to the average customer, who will not specifically point out these topics.

**Important:**
- **Let the customers talk, not the interviewer!**
- **Don't sell your own idea!**

### Competency level for (technology) knowledge of the persona

| Competency Level | Description |
|---|---|
| **0 – no knowledge** | No knowledge |
| **1 – Newbie** | Has heard of the topic and knows where to find more information. Uncertainty in the subject. |
| **2 – basic knowledge** | Meets the basic requirements. Can work on simple tasks with initial support. |
| **3 – Advanced knowledge** | Can work on standard tasks themselves. |
| **4 – Consolidated knowledge** | Can work on standard tasks themselves. Independently develops new solutions for standard tasks. |
| **5 – Expert Knowledge** | Specialist in his field. Can handle complex tasks himself. Constantly develops the state of the art using new approaches. |

*Table: Competency level for the (technology) knowledge of the persona.*

## (3) Design Principles & Persona

**Content: Synthesis of the first two steps.**
- **Find patterns** → What is the **common sense** (shared traits) between the customers?
- Create a **profile of the person**, e.g. using **"Personas"** or **"Point-of-View"**.
- **Visualize** the customer's point of view.
- **Update your "(1) Understand"**:
  - What is the problem?
  - What's the target, including functions and features needed?

### Persona

- A **persona** is a concept from **analytical psychology** to describe the **outer view and acting** of a person.
- From Greek / Latin — similar to a **face / role**.

### Buyer Persona

- An **adaptation of the persona to economics** to **describe and understand the customer**.
- A **fictional person** that best describes the **target groups and customers** of a product.
- Target: describe the customer **as best as possible** with all their **attributes, knowledge, background and behaviours** — *like a good actress*.
- Helps you **take decisions in the name of the fictional customer** — would he/she like it, use it, can deal with it…?
- ➜ **Imagine you write a novel and need to describe the person.**

### Persona template (the fields to fill in)

A persona card collects:

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Demographics** (fictional but fitting) | Name · Sex · Age · Domicile · Relationship status · Kids · Job · Income · Education |
| **Photo / Drawing** | A matching picture of the persona |
| **Technology** | Knowledge about **5 relevant core technologies** |
| **Motivates** | What makes the persona happy? Spare-time interests / hobbies (×3) |
| **Frustrates** | What bothers / angers the persona? How could this impact the product? (×3) |
| **Goals** | The goals / targets of the persona (×3) |
| **Pain Points / Problems** | The pain point / problem the persona needs solved (×3) |

#### Example persona — "Jack Johnsen"

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | Jack Johnsen |
| Sex | Male |
| Age | 54 |
| Domicile | Baltic Sea, owner-occupied home |
| Relationship status | Married |
| Kids | 3 |
| Job | Lifeguard |
| Income | 3,150 € per month |
| Education | Apprenticeship as Paramedic |

- **Technology:** Smartphone · Social Networks · Programming · UX Design · Painting
- **Motivates:** Loves fresh air · Working with kids · Surfing
- **Frustrates:** Bad weather · Empty smartphone · Littering by tourists
- **Goals:** Ride the biggest wave in the Baltic Sea · Protect the environment · Raise his kids
- **Pain Points / Problems:** Find the biggest wave · Enable people to take away their own garbage

## (4) Ideation

**Content: Create ideas to solve the customer's pain points.**
- There are **NO boundaries** (not about costs, technical limitations, …) — **the crazier, the better the idea!**
- **Consolidation** of the ideas.
- **Decision making.**
- **Get feedback** from the customer.
- If necessary, **competitor analysis**.

**How to:**
- **Individual** creative creation of new ideas.
- **Gathering and discussion** of all ideas.
- Decisions made via **decision-making tools**, e.g.:
  - **Morphological box**
  - **QFD** (Quality Function Deployment)
- **Get feedback** from the customer.

> Decision criteria used to select ideas include **practicability**, **efficiency** and **economy**.

---

## (5) Prototyping

**Content: Create a tangible prototype.**
- A **physical prototype that can be experienced** is created.
- **Everything is allowed** for construction — be creative!
- **Premise: the simpler, the better!**

**Important:**
- **NO verbal explanation** to the customer of what it should do!
- The **customer must understand the solution** — not you!
- It has to **solve the customer's problem**, not realize *your* idea!

**How to:** Be creative!

---

## (6) Testing

**Content:**
- **Testing of the prototype by the customer.**
- The target is to **get feedback.**
- If the customer does not understand the idea, or if it does not work, the idea can be **changed or even discarded** in the next cycle.
- **Observe the customer** during the tests and note what needs to be improved for the next development cycle.
- **Take several iterations!**

**How To:**
- **No demonstration** to the customer!
- **Visual observation** of the customer and his/her usage of the product.
- **Take several iterations!**

## Worked Example (MRI)

- Example of **successful design thinking**: an **MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)** machine was **scary for children**, so it was **redesigned** [ON_DesignThinking_Uboot].
- By understanding the *real* customer need (a child who is frightened), the scanner room was reimagined (e.g. as a **pirate adventure / submarine** theme) — solving the customer's problem instead of just the technical one.

## Exercise

A 5-team workshop runs the full design thinking process hands-on.

**Initial setup:**
- Create **5 teams** (count to 5); each team gathers at one table.
- Each team takes **one set of workshop material**; each person gets a stack of **paper templates** for design thinking.
- After the exercise — **clean up the workshop material properly!**

**Process & timing:**

| Step | Activity | Time |
|---|---|---|
| (1) | Understand — describe the problem you want to solve | 10 min |
| (2) | Observe — interview **min. 2 customers** (from other groups, **not your own**); briefly describe the problem only | 10 min |
| (3) | Design Principles — present interview data, create persona(s), update "(1) Understand" | 15 min |
| (4a) | Ideation – **create**: individual **drawing/painting** (NO writing, NO exchange); min. 5 (better 10) ideas | 15 min |
| (4b) | Ideation – **select**: share ideas in group, take/modify best 5, decide via "system" (e.g. morphological box) | 15 min |
| (4c) | Ideation – **feedback**: present the 5 **group** ideas to customer(s); note what they like/dislike | 15 min |
| (5) | Prototyping — pick **2 ideas** from feedback, build **physical prototypes** | 30 min |
| (6) | Testing — hand over the product **without explanation**, observe, take notes | 10 min |
| (7) | **Iterate** — go back to steps 5+6, update and re-test | 20 min |
| (8) | **Presentation** to the audience | 10 min per team |

**Total:** 140 min (base) + 10 min per team = **190 min**.

**Interview ("Observe") rules:** everybody interviews **min. 2× customers** (e.g. students randomly picked from other groups); **don't interview your own group**; describe the problem briefly — **don't explain a detailed solution.**

**Ideation – create rules:** individual drawing/painting of possible solutions · **NO writing** · **NO exchange** in the group · min. 5 (better 10) ideas · use only the "Drawing" boxes, not "Feedback".

**Ideation – select criteria:** practicability · efficiency · economy …

**Presentation should answer:** What was the problem? · Who are your personas? · What ideas did you go through? · What was the "final" solution?

**Method feedback:** What was good about this method? · What was hard about this method?

## Summary

- **Design thinking is a customer-centred method for designing products.**
- In **fast and iterative cycles**, the **needs and problems of a customer** can be **analysed and solved** with several ideas.
- Core mindset: keep the **customer at the centre**, **fail fast and cheap**, and **iterate** until the prototype truly solves the customer's problem.

## Glossary / terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Design Thinking** | Agile, iterative method for developing creative ideas and solving problems, centred on the customer. |
| **Problem Space** | Phase of understanding the problem and the customer (steps 1–2). |
| **Solution Space** | Phase of creating and testing solutions (steps 4–6). |
| **Fail fast and cheap** | Iterate quickly with cheap prototypes so mistakes are caught early and cheaply. |
| **Persona** | Concept from analytical psychology describing the outer view/role of a person. |
| **Buyer Persona** | Fictional but fitting person representing the target customer, used to decide "in their name". |
| **Point-of-View** | A way to capture/visualize the customer's perspective (alternative to a persona). |
| **Competency level (0–5)** | Scale rating the persona's knowledge of a technology (0 none → 5 expert). |
| **Morphological box** | Decision-making/idea-combination tool used to select ideas. |
| **QFD** | Quality Function Deployment — a decision-making tool. |
| **Prototype** | A simple, tangible, experienceable model of the solution. |
| **Iteration** | Repeating the prototype-test loop to improve the solution. |
| **Desirability/Feasibility/Viability** | The three foci: customer benefit · possibility to realize · commercial viability. |
